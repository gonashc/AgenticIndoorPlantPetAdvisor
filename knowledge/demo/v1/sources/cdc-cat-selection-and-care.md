# General cat selection, household fit, and care

CDC guidance recommends researching a cat's needs before adoption and choosing a cat whose
personality and activity level fit the household, the animals already living there, and the time
available for care. A prospective owner should ask about appropriate food and the environment the
cat needs. A newly adopted cat or kitten should see a veterinarian within a few days to a week,
including when the animal appears healthy.

Cats need a safe, comfortable home, daily access to suitable food and fresh water, and clean litter
facilities. CDC recommends one litter box for each cat plus one additional box. Ongoing care
includes regular veterinary visits, recommended vaccinations, and veterinarian-directed prevention
for fleas, ticks, heartworms, and other parasites. A cat's indoor or outdoor lifestyle and contact
with other animals should be discussed with the veterinarian. Tick products should not be applied
to a cat without veterinary guidance because cats can be unusually sensitive to some chemicals.

Household health practices include washing hands after handling cats, their dishes and supplies,
or litter waste; changing litter boxes daily; keeping litter areas away from children and food
preparation; and supervising children around cats. Pregnant people and people with weakened immune
systems should avoid litter-box cleaning when possible. Rough play should be avoided, and bites or
scratches should be washed promptly, with medical advice sought when the wound or animal presents
risk signs.

This source supports general selection and care considerations shared by this application's three
Cat profiles. It does not establish breed behavior, predict an individual cat's temperament,
differentiate the application's age-based profiles, or assign compatibility scores.
